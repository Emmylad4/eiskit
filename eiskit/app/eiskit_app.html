How to set up your data

One row per frequency, three columns: frequency (Hz) · real part Z′ (Ω) · imaginary part (Ω). A header row is optional. Tabs, commas, semicolons or spaces are all fine, and decimal commas (2,549) are understood.

Frequency (Hz)   Z' (Ohm)   -Z'' (Ohm)
100000           2.549      0.383
10000            2.561      0.081
...              ...        ...

Imaginary sign: instruments export either Z″ (negative for capacitive data) or −Z″. A header such as -Z'' or -Im(Z) is recognised; without a header the sign is taken from the data. You can also set it explicitly.

Several samples in one file: put the three-column blocks next to each other (an empty column between them is fine) and the sample name in the cell above each block.

No need to delete odd points. The fit is robust: it follows the trend of the data and automatically down-weights points that deviate from it (noise spikes, instrument artefacts). Those points are drawn hollow and listed in the results.

Searches fit every circuit and rank them: circuits whose parameters are all determined come first, then by AICc (fit quality penalised for the number of parameters). The common circuit has the lowest summed AICc over all samples among circuits valid for at least half of them – use it when parameters must be compared between samples.

Fit

Distribution of relaxation times

Model-free analysis: the spectrum is decomposed into relaxation processes, γ(τ) ≥ 0, by Tikhonov-regularised non-negative least squares. Each peak is a process (f = 1/2πτ) and its area is that process's resistance. A series capacitance is added automatically for blocking spectra that do not return to the real axis.

How the fit works

Complex non-linear least squares: real and imaginary parts are fitted together, each point weighted by 1/|Z| (every frequency decade counts equally). Parameters stay physical (R, C, Q, τ > 0; 0.3 ≤ n ≤ 1). Several starting points are tried.

Robust fitting: a soft-L1 fit followed by a Cauchy (redescending) fit whose scale is estimated from the median deviation. Points that follow the trend are fitted normally; points far from it (more than 4 robust standard deviations) are down-weighted, shown hollow, and excluded from the statistics. No manual deletion is needed.

Uncertainties: standard errors from the covariance s²(JᵀJ)⁻¹; 95 % CI = value ± 1.96·SD. A fit is flagged if any parameter is undetermined (relative error > 100 %), at a bound, or physically meaningless (infinite/negligible resistance).

Model selection: valid fits first, then lowest AICc. Choose among the best few with physical knowledge of your system.

Kramers–Kronig: linear Lin-KK test (Schönleber et al., 2014); max residual < 1 % = PASS, < 2 % = OK.

DRT: Tikhonov regularisation with a second-derivative penalty and non-negativity (cf. Wan et al., 2015); λ chosen as the smoothest solution whose reconstruction error is within 25 % of the best attainable.

Effective capacitance of an R∥CPE: Ceff = Q1/nR(1−n)/n (Hsu & Mansfeld, 2001).
